### About the dataset

#### Purpose

The purpose of this interactive bar chart is to explore how diabetes prevalence changes across different age groups. Hover over the points to see the exact count.

-   **Graph Explanation**: Diabetes Outcomes by Age Group x-axis: Age Group (years): Patients are grouped into 5-year intervals (20–24, 25–29, up to 80–85), allowing us to track how diabetes prevalence changes across different life stages. y-axis: Number of Individuals: The count of patients in each age group, split by diabetes diagnosis outcome.

-   **The bars**: Each bar represents the count of patients in each age group. When viewing "All" outcomes, bars are displayed side by side — blue for non-diabetic and orange for Diabetic — allowing direct comparison within each age group. When a single outcome is selected, only one set of bars is shown.

-   **Total Individuals in Range**: The annotation in the top-left corner shows the total number of patients currently visible and is updated dynamically as the age slider is adjusted.

#### What does this plot show?

1.  **Diabetes prevalence increases with age.** In younger age groups (20–30), non-diabetic individuals outnumber diabetic ones by a wide margin. As age increases, the gap narrows and the proportion of diabetic cases grow steadily.
2.  **Peak risk appears in the 40–55 age range.** This is where the diabetic count is highest relative to the overall group size, suggesting middle age is the period of greatest diabetes risk in this population.
3.  **The oldest age groups show smaller counts overall.** This reflects the natural reduction in sample size at older ages in the dataset, not necessarily a drop in diabetes risk. ####Further analysis using the slider Diabetes Outcome dropdown: Filter by All, Diabetic, or Non-diabetic to isolate a specific group. Age Range slider: Narrow the age window to zoom into a specific life stage. Sliding toward younger ages (20–35) shows that non-diabetic cases dominate heavily, while sliding toward older ages (45+) reveals a shrinking gap between the two groups, reflecting rising diabetes risk with age.
